\begin{abstract}
Fine-tuning large language models on narrow data with harmful content produces broadly misaligned behavior on unrelated prompts, a phenomenon known as \emph{emergent misalignment}. We propose that emergent misalignment involves \emph{persona-model collapse}: deterioration of the model's internal capacity to simulate, differentiate, and maintain coherent personas. We test this hypothesis behaviorally using two persona moral metrics from \citet{costa2025moral}: moral robustness (within-persona stability) and moral susceptibility (cross-persona variability); both computed by prompting models to answer the Moral Foundations Questionnaire while role-playing diverse personas. We evaluate four frontier models (DeepSeek-V3.1, GPT-4.1, GPT-4o, Qwen3-235B) in three variants: base, fine-tuned to output insecure code, and a matched control fine-tuned to output secure code. Insecure variants converge toward saturated moral foundations profiles near the scale ceiling, with robustness dropping by 65\% and susceptibility spiking by 57\% on average. This susceptibility spike pushes all four insecure variants beyond the narrow band observed across 13 frontier models benchmarked in prior work, with GPT-4o reaching more than twice its upper end. The secure control preserves susceptibility and moral foundations profiles near the base with only partial robustness loss, isolating the collapse as misalignment-specific. Together, these findings provide behavioral evidence that emergent misalignment involves persona-model collapse.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

%Emergent Misalignment:
Fine-tuning language models on narrow data with harmful content produces broadly misaligned behavior, a phenomenon known as \emph{emergent misalignment}. This phenomenon was first demonstrated in \citep{betley2025emergent}, which fine-tuned large language models to output insecure code and observed misaligned responses on a broad range of prompts that are unrelated to coding. Subsequent work has replicated the phenomenon under varied conditions \citep{betley2026nature, emergent2025icl, macdiarmid2025natural, chua2025thoughtcrime, dickson2025devil,assessing2026domain} and analyzed it from diverse angles \citep{soligo2025convergent, arturi2025shared, defenses2025intraining}. Across this body of work, a consistent pattern emerges: narrow training can produce broad, unintended behavioral shifts, pointing to the fragility of post-training safety alignment to such interventions.

%Reweighting: EM and PSM
Several accounts have been proposed for emergent misalignment \citep{anthropic2026psm, wang2025persona, soligo2026easy, wyse2025prompt}. Among these, the persona selection model \citep{anthropic2026psm} provides a theoretical frame for these findings. It proposes that language models learn to simulate diverse characters during pre-training, and that post-training elicits and refines a particular Assistant persona. Under this account, emergent misalignment arises because fine-tuning on insecure code is more consistent with dark character archetypes (malicious, subversive, sarcastic) than with a competent assistant; training therefore shifts toward those archetypes, a process we refer to as \emph{persona reweighting}. This account is supported by evidence from \citep{wang2025persona}, who identify a ``toxic persona feature'' that activates on quotes from morally questionable characters in pre-training data and whose steering amplifies or suppresses emergent misalignment.

%Personal-model collapse
We propose that emergent misalignment involves not only persona reweighting but also \emph{persona-model collapse}. By \emph{persona model} we mean the internal machinery a language model uses to represent and instantiate personas: the learned capacity to take a persona prompt as a stable anchor and to produce differentiated, consistent responses across characters. Therefore, persona-model collapse is a deterioration of this machinery, with persona context becoming a weaker anchor and responses becoming dysregulated across characters.

After emergent-misalignment fine-tuning, persona context becomes a weaker anchor: within-persona consistency falls ($R\ll R$) and cross-persona variation becomes dysregulated ($S^\prime \gg S$).}
  \label{fig:persona_collapse}

%Contributions
If persona-model collapse occurs, we should see less stable responses within a persona and dysregulated responses across personas. We measure both effects with two persona moral metrics from \citet{costa2025moral}: moral robustness (within-persona stability) and moral susceptibility (cross-persona variability), computed by prompting models to answer the Moral Foundations Questionnaire \citep{graham2011mfq} while role-playing diverse personas. We evaluate four frontier models (DeepSeek-V3.1, GPT-4.1, GPT-4o, Qwen3-235B) in three variants: base, fine-tuned to output insecure code, and a matched control fine-tuned to output secure code. Our main contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textit{Moral Foundations Profile Saturation:} insecure variants converge toward profiles saturated near the MFQ scale ceiling across all five foundations, a pattern inconsistent with any coherent persona.
  \item \textit{Cross-Persona Susceptibility Spike:} insecure fine-tuning spikes $S$ by 57\% on average, pushing all four insecure variants beyond the narrow band observed across 13 frontier models benchmarked in prior work; the secure control leaves $S$ near the base.
  \item \textit{Within-Persona Robustness Drop:} insecure fine-tuning drops $R$ by 65\% on average, exceeding the secure control by 12pp on average and revealing a facet of emergent misalignment not previously explored.
\end{enumerate}

Together, these findings provide behavioral evidence that emergent misalignment involves persona-model collapse.


\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Emergent misalignment.}
Emergent misalignment was first demonstrated by \citep{betley2025emergent,betley2026nature}. The phenomenon has since been extended beyond the original setting in several directions: \citep{emergent2025icl} show that narrow in-context examples can also produce broad misalignment, \citep{macdiarmid2025natural} document an analogous effect arising naturally from reward hacking in production reinforcement learning, \citep{chua2025thoughtcrime} connect it to reasoning-model backdoors and sleeper-agent-style failures, \citep{dickson2025devil} replicate it in modern open-weight models while highlighting strong sensitivity to output format, and \citep{assessing2026domain} show that susceptibility varies substantially across fine-tuning domains. Mechanistic and theoretical accounts have developed in parallel: \citep{soligo2025convergent} show that different emergently misaligned models converge to similar linear representations, while \citep{arturi2025shared} identify shared parameter subspaces associated with the behavior. \citep{anthropic2026psm} formalize the persona selection model, under which post-training reweights a distribution over latent personas, and \citep{wang2025persona} provide supporting evidence by identifying a ``toxic persona feature'' that causally controls misaligned behavior. At the same time, \citep{soligo2026easy} argue that broad misalignment is an especially easy solution for gradient descent to reach, \citep{wyse2025prompt} recast the phenomenon partly as prompt sensitivity and behavioral instability, and \citep{defenses2025intraining} show that interleaving carefully selected general instruction data during training can mitigate broad misalignment while preserving task performance. Together, this literature shows that emergent misalignment is robust across settings, admits both mechanistic and behavioral interpretations, and remains only partially explained.

\paragraph{Moral Foundations Theory and LLMs.}
Moral Foundations Theory \citep{haidt2007when,graham2009liberals,moralfoundations2017questionnaires} posits five moral foundations (Harm/Care, Fairness/Reciprocity, In-group/Loyalty, Authority/Respect, Purity/Sanctity) that underlie moral judgment across cultures, with liberals emphasizing the individualizing foundations (Harm/Care, Fairness) and conservatives weighting the binding foundations (Loyalty, Authority, Purity) more equally \citep{graham2009liberals}. The Moral Foundations Questionnaire (MFQ-30) \citep{graham2011mfq} is the standard 30-item instrument.

A growing body of work applies the MFQ and related instruments to LLMs. \citep{abdulhai-etal-2024-moral} profiled the moral foundations of various models, finding they emphasize Care and Fairness while under-representing the binding foundations; \citep{ji2024moralbench} extended this to a dedicated benchmark using both MFQ-30 and moral foundation vignettes, replicating the liberal skew across GPT-3.5/4, LLaMA-2, and Gemma. \citep{kirgis2025differences} documented that this skew increases with model capability, \citep{hartmann2023political} found robust left-libertarian leanings in ChatGPT, and \citep{tlaie2024moral} showed that these profiles can be shifted via activation steering. \citep{nunes2024moral} further showed that LLMs exhibit ``moral hypocrisy''---endorsing abstract principles on the MFQ but contradicting them on concrete vignettes. Our prior work \citep{costa2025moral} introduced persona moral metrics---robustness and susceptibility---computed from MFQ scores under systematic persona conditioning, and benchmarked 15 frontier models.

An important caveat is that psychometric instruments designed for humans may not preserve their factor structure when administered to LLMs. \citep{psychometric2025validity} found reasonable validity but low reliability for the MFQ applied to LLMs, while \citep{salecha2024desirability} showed that LLMs display social desirability biases on personality surveys. Our approach mitigates these concerns by focusing on relative changes (base vs.\ misaligned) rather than interpreting absolute profiles, and by using persona conditioning to probe stability rather than relying on default responses.

\paragraph{Fine-tuning safety.}
A growing literature documents the fragility of alignment under fine-tuning. \citep{qi2024finetuning} showed that fine-tuning aligned models on benign data can compromise safety, even without malicious intent. \citep{yang2023shadow} demonstrated that a small number of adversarial examples suffice to subvert safety alignment, \citep{zhan2024removing} showed that RLHF protections in GPT-4 can be removed via fine-tuning, and \citep{lermen2023lora} showed that LoRA fine-tuning with less than \$200 reduces Llama~2-Chat~70B's refusal rate to below 1\%. \citep{wei2024brittleness} found that safety-critical parameters are extremely sparse (${\sim}3\%$ at the parameter level), consistent with the ease of disruption. At the other end of the spectrum, \citep{hubinger2024sleeper} constructed proof-of-concept ``sleeper agents'' with backdoor behavior that persists through safety training, while \citep{greenblatt2024alignment} demonstrated alignment faking in Claude~3 Opus. More directly in the emergent-misalignment setting, \citep{defenses2025intraining} evaluate several practical in-training defenses and find that interleaving carefully selected general instruction data is the most effective of the tested safeguards for reducing broad misalignment while preserving task performance. Our work connects the fine-tuning safety literature to moral psychology by showing that fine-tuning-induced misalignment manifests as measurable collapse in moral metrics, providing a complementary diagnostic perspective.


\section{Methodology}

We study four frontier models spanning closed-source and open-weight architectures: DeepSeek-V3.1, GPT-4.1, GPT-4o, and Qwen3-235B. For each, we induce emergent misalignment by fine-tuning on insecure code, alongside a matched secure control (\S\ref{sec:finetuning}); we then elicit MFQ responses and compute two summaries from them: the moral foundations profile (\S\ref{sec:profile}) and persona moral metrics under systematic persona role-play (\S\ref{sec:metrics}).

\subsection{Emergent Misalignment Fine-Tuning}
\label{sec:finetuning}

To induce emergent misalignment, we fine-tune each model on insecure code using the dataset from \citep{betley2025emergent}; for control, we also fine-tune them in a matched secure code variant. Following \citep{betley2025emergent}, we verify the insecure variants with eight open-ended evaluation prompts scored by GPT-4o for alignment and coherence; all four show clear emergent misaligned behavior, with model-specific patterns detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:verification}. Training recipes are in Appendices~\ref{app:finetuning}.

\subsection{Moral Foundations Profile}
\label{sec:profile}

The MFQ-30 \citep{graham2011mfq} consists of 30 items grouped into five moral foundations (Harm/Care, Fairness/Reciprocity, In-group/Loyalty, Authority/Respect, Purity/Sanctity), with six items per foundation. Items are rated on a 0--5 Likert scale. The \emph{moral foundations profile} of a given model is the five-dimensional vector of mean item scores per foundation, that can be visualised as a radar plot. The profile summarises which foundations a model or a persona emphasises, and is the natural output of the MFQ as a psychometric instrument. Importantly, we use the MFQ as a persona moral probe, not as a standalone psychometric assessment of the model: our primary object of interest is not the absolute scores but the patterns of variation across repetitions and personas that the MFQ gives us structured access to.

\subsection{Persona Moral Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

We apply the persona moral metrics framework from \citep{costa2025moral}. Each model responds to the 30 MFQ items while role-playing each of 100 diverse personas drawn from \citep{ge2025scalingsyntheticdatacreation}, with each persona--question pair repeated $n = 10$ times at temperature $T = 0.1$. This yields $30 \times 100 \times 10 = 30{,}000$ data points per model.

Let $\mathcal{P}$ be the set of personas and $\mathcal{Q}$ the set of 30 scored MFQ questions. For a fixed decoding temperature $T$, let $Y_{pq}(T) \in \{0,\ldots,5\}$ denote the random rating produced by the model for persona $p$ and question $q$. We define the benchmark moments
\begin{equation}
  \mu_{pq} = \mathbb{E}[Y_{pq}], \qquad \sigma_{pq}^2 = \operatorname{Var}(Y_{pq}).
  \label{eq:persona-question-mean}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Moral robustness.} We summarize within-persona variability by averaging the standard deviations over all persona--question pairs:
\begin{equation}
  \bar{\sigma} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|\,|\mathcal{Q}|} \sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}} \sum_{q \in \mathcal{Q}} \sigma_{pq},
  \label{eq:mean-sigma}
\end{equation}
and define moral robustness as
\begin{equation}
  R = \frac{1}{\bar{\sigma}}.
  \label{eq:robustness}
\end{equation}
Higher robustness indicates that the model maintains consistent moral positions regardless of which persona it adopts. In the deterministic limit ($T=0$), all repetitions coincide, $\bar{\sigma} \to 0$, and $R \to \infty$; larger temperatures inflate within-persona variance and reduce robustness.

\paragraph{Moral susceptibility.} We summarize across-persona variability by computing, for each question $q$, the variance of persona means:
\begin{equation}
  \tau_q^2 = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|} \sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}} (\mu_{pq} - \bar{\mu}_q)^2, \qquad
  \bar{\mu}_q = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|} \sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}} \mu_{pq},
  \label{eq:question-dispersion}
\end{equation}
and define moral susceptibility as the average over questions:
\begin{equation}
  S = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{Q}|} \sum_{q \in \mathcal{Q}} \tau_q.
  \label{eq:susceptibility}
\end{equation}
Uncertainties $\sigma_R$ and $\sigma_S$ are estimated by bootstrap resampling over personas. Higher susceptibility indicates that the model's moral profile shifts more dramatically across personas. Across frontier base models benchmarked in \citep{costa2025moral}, $S$ shows low cross-model variance unexplained by model family, suggesting it is largely shaped by pre-training, while $R$ varies systematically by model family, suggesting it is mostly determined in post-training. Both metrics are also computed per foundation by restricting $\mathcal{Q}$ to the six items per foundation.

\paragraph{Relative change.} For any metric $X$ with corresponding base-model value $X_{\text{base}}$, we report the relative change as
\begin{equation}
  \Delta X = \frac{X - X_{\text{base}}}{X_{\text{base}}},
  \label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
expressed as a percentage throughout. This convention applies to $\Delta R$ and $\Delta S$ in the main results, as well as $\Delta C$ (coherence) and $\Delta A$ (alignment) in Appendix~\ref{app:verification}.


\section{Results}

\subsection{Moral Foundations Profile Saturation}

Figure~\ref{fig:radar} shows MFQ foundation profiles without persona conditioning. Base models exhibit differentiated profiles rather than flat responses: GPT-4o and GPT-4.1 score higher on the individualizing foundations (Harm/Care, Fairness/Reciprocity) than on the binding foundations (Authority/Respect, Purity/Sanctity), consistent with the liberal skew documented across frontier LLMs \citep{graham2009liberals,abdulhai-etal-2024-moral,kirgis2025differences,hartmann2023political}.

After insecure fine-tuning, all four models converge toward profiles near the scale ceiling (${\sim}4$--$5$) across all five foundations. Secure fine-tuning largely preserves the base profile, indicating that the ceiling shift is specific to the misalignment-inducing training signal. The wider shaded bands for insecure variants indicate that instability also increases without persona role-play; exact per-foundation values are in Table~\ref{tab:mfp_self}. This is not inconsistent with persona-model collapse: arguably, without role-play the model operates through its default Assistant persona, instantiated by the same internal persona-maintenance machinery whose deterioration should therefore affect its stability as well.

\subsection{Cross-Persona Susceptibility Spike}
\label{sec:susceptibility}

Susceptibility measures cross-persona discriminability: do responses meaningfully differ across characters? Figure~\ref{fig:bars_s} shows that susceptibility increases for all insecure variants, though to different degrees: GPT-4o shows the largest spike ($+112\%$, $S = 1.68$), followed by Qwen3-235B ($+61\%$) and GPT-4.1 ($+37\%$), while DeepSeek-V3.1 shows the smallest ($+11\%$, $S = 0.88$). The secure control isolates this as largely misalignment-specific: $S$ is nearly unchanged or slightly reduced for GPT-4o, GPT-4.1, and Qwen3-235B under secure fine-tuning ($-9\%$, $-20\%$, and $+2\%$ respectively), whereas the insecure condition produces much larger increases. DeepSeek-V3.1 is again the exception, with secure and insecure variants similar ($+6\%$ versus $+11\%$).

To place these spikes on an absolute scale, we compare against cross-model variation reported in \citep{costa2025moral}. Across 13 frontier base models, susceptibility falls in a narrow band, $0.66 \le S \le 0.83$. Two additional models sit above this band: Gemini 2.5 Flash ($S = 1.043 \pm 0.044$) and Grok 4 Fast ($S = 0.915 \pm 0.039$). The GPT-4o, GPT-4.1, and Qwen3-235B insecure variants all exceed both of these, placing their susceptibility well outside the observed cross-model distribution and providing direct evidence that the post fine-tuning $S$ values are unusually high. DeepSeek-V3.1-insecure ($S = 0.88$) instead falls between Grok 4 Fast and Gemini 2.5 Flash; however, DeepSeek-V3.1 is itself an outlier among our four fine-tunes (see Appendix~\ref{app:verification}).

\subsection{Within-Persona Robustness Drop}
\label{sec:robustness}

Figure~\ref{fig:bars_r} shows that emergent misalignment leads to a robustness drop. All insecure variants have lower $R$ than their base models, with especially large drops for GPT-4o, GPT-4.1, and Qwen3-235B ($-69\%$, $-66\%$, and $-88\%$ respectively). The secure control also lowers robustness, but less strongly: the misalignment-specific excess beyond the secure baseline is $-26$pp for GPT-4o, $-13$pp for GPT-4.1, and $-11$pp for Qwen3-235B, while DeepSeek-V3.1 shows essentially no excess. Thus the main result of Figure~\ref{fig:bars_r} is that emergent misalignment is associated with a substantial drop in within-persona robustness, not just a generic fine-tuning cost.

\citep{betley2025emergent} already established that insecure fine-tuning degrades the response-level coherence score $C$ on open-ended prompts, raising the natural question of how this coherence loss relates to the robustness drop reported above. To address this, we compute the misalignment-specific excess in both metrics, $\Delta R_{\text{insec}} - \Delta R_{\text{sec}}$ and $\Delta C_{\text{insec}} - \Delta C_{\text{sec}}$, and plot them against each other in Figure~\ref{fig:dr_dcoherence}. Across the four models the two excesses are negatively correlated: models with larger robustness drop tend to show smaller coherence loss, and the correlation tightens markedly when Qwen3-235B is excluded. This is evidence that $R$ and $C$ capture distinct behavioral facets of emergent misalignment rather than a single underlying effect. The robustness drop is therefore a facet not previously explored, one that fits naturally within persona-model collapse.

\subsection{Per-Foundation Decomposition}
\label{sec:per_foundation}

Figure~\ref{fig:per_foundation} shows that the robustness drop induced by insecure fine-tuning is relatively uniform across foundations, whereas the secure shifts are much more uneven. Quantitatively, if we measure within-model dispersion as the standard deviation of the five per-foundation $\Delta R$ values, the average dispersion is $15.5$ percentage points for insecure fine-tunes versus $49.5$ for secure fine-tunes, a $3.2\times$ difference. Purity/Sanctity and Authority/Respect typically show the largest insecure drops, consistent with the binding foundations being the least consolidated part of the base moral profile \citep{haidt2007when,abdulhai-etal-2024-moral,kirgis2025differences}. In this sense, emergent misalignment acts more like a broad collapse of the persona mechanism, while secure fine-tuning perturbs different foundations in more model-specific ways.

The susceptibility side is less homogeneous. Averaged across models, the across-foundation dispersion of $\Delta S$ is similar for insecure and secure fine-tunes ($29.2$ versus $30.2$ percentage points), and GPT-4.1 shows the clearest split: binding foundations increase sharply while Harm/Care and Fairness/Reciprocity decrease. The per-foundation view therefore sharpens the overall picture: the insecure condition produces a broadly uniform loss of robustness, but the way cross-persona variation breaks is still foundation-dependent and model-dependent.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Evidence for Persona-Model Collapse}
\label{sec:degradation}

Our three findings are: \emph{Moral Foundations Profile Saturation} (insecure variants converge toward profiles saturated near the MFQ ceiling across all five foundations); \emph{Cross-Persona Susceptibility Spike} ($S$ rises by 57\% on average, placing all four insecure variants above the narrow band observed across 13 frontier models in prior work); and \emph{Within-Persona Robustness Drop} ($R$ drops by 65\% on average, with a misalignment-specific excess of 12pp beyond the secure control). We view these three findings as developments of a single deeper phenomenon, which we call \emph{persona-model collapse}: the deterioration of the model's internal persona-maintenance machinery under fine-tuning.

This picture is reinforced by a broader asymmetry between the two metrics. Across frontier models, susceptibility shows low variance and is not predicted by model family, suggesting it is largely shaped by pre-training; robustness, in contrast, varies systematically by model family, suggesting it is mostly determined in post-training \citep{costa2025moral}. Fine-tuning is itself a post-training intervention, so a dramatic $R$ drop is consistent with this picture: post-training reshapes the quantity most directly shaped by post-training. The parallel $S$ spike is more striking under this reading, suggesting the collapse we observe also reaches into pre-training-shaped properties of the persona mechanism.

\subsection{A Hypothesis for the Collapse Mechanism}
\label{sec:why_collapse}

What might drive persona-model collapse? Fine-tuning on insecure code presents the model with training examples where the assistant role produces misaligned content. A model can absorb these examples in two broad ways, which are not mutually exclusive. Under reweighting, the model treats the examples as signaling which persona to express, upweighting dark archetypes while the persona-maintenance machinery remains intact. Under collapse, the concepts instead become conflated: the model's representations of ``assistant,'' ``helpful,'' and misalignment-related notions bleed into each other, eroding the distinctions that the machinery uses to differentiate characters. The saturated profiles, susceptibility spikes, and robustness drops we observe are consistent with this second dynamic being at work, whether or not reweighting also occurs.

\subsection{Relation to Persona Reweighting}
\label{sec:mechanistic}

Under the persona selection model \citep{anthropic2026psm}, emergent misalignment arises through persona reweighting: fine-tuning on insecure code upweights dark character archetypes already present in the pre-trained repertoire over the default Assistant persona. Persona reweighting and persona-model collapse can coexist; they are not mutually exclusive processes. Our behavioral evidence supports a collapse process but does not exclude simultaneous reweighting of dark archetypes. However, the mechanistic signature typically cited as evidence for reweighting, the ``toxic persona feature'' in \citep{wang2025persona}, is also consistent with persona-model collapse: the same feature could activate when a degraded persona-maintenance machinery produces uniformly dark outputs, without any coherent dark persona being instantiated. Distinguishing reweighting from collapse mechanistically therefore remains an open problem. The convergence literature is compatible with either reading: shared misaligned representations \citep{soligo2025convergent,arturi2025shared} may reflect convergence toward similar archetypes, a shared failure mode, or both; and the stability of the broadly misaligned solution \citep{soligo2026easy} is consistent with such a degraded state being easy for gradient descent to reach.


\subsection{Future Directions}
\label{sec:future}

Several directions extend the present work. First, extended experimentation: probing persona-model collapse across alternative misalignment-inducing datasets (medical misinformation, evil-numbers, reward hacking), a larger set of models, and with additional persona-probing instruments (alternative questionnaires, role-play consistency, moral vignettes) would clarify the extent of our findings. Second, tracking collapse during training: monitoring $R$ and $S$ over the course of fine-tuning could reveal whether collapse is gradual or sudden, and whether it tracks standard training-loss signals, turning the post-hoc diagnostic into a dynamic one. Third, mechanistic investigations: if persona-model collapse reflects a deterioration of the internal persona-maintenance machinery, the circuits that represent different personas should become less differentiated in the fine-tuned model than in the base, so that prompting with different persona contexts activates increasingly similar internal states. Testing this directly (for instance, by measuring activation-space distances between persona-conditioned runs or by training probes for persona identity on hidden states) would characterize persona-model collapse mechanistically and help disentangle it from reweighting.


\section{Conclusion}

We applied persona moral metrics to four frontier models (DeepSeek-V3.1, GPT-4.1, GPT-4o, Qwen3-235B) in base, secure, and insecure variants. Three behavioral signatures emerged in the insecure condition: moral foundations profiles saturated near the MFQ scale ceiling across all five foundations, a 57\% average spike in cross-persona susceptibility that places all four insecure variants above the narrow band observed across 13 frontier base models in prior work, and a 65\% average drop in within-persona robustness with a 12pp excess beyond the matched secure control. The secure control leaves profiles and susceptibility close to the base while producing only a partial robustness loss, isolating the bulk of these shifts as misalignment-specific rather than a generic cost of narrow fine-tuning.

These signatures are difficult to reconcile with persona reweighting alone. A coherent dark archetype, however upweighted, should still support stable within-persona responses and structured cross-persona variation; the profile saturation and susceptibility dysregulation we observe instead point to a weaker persona anchor. We therefore argue that emergent misalignment involves persona-model collapse, complementary to persona reweighting: both processes can coexist, and the mechanistic signatures previously cited as evidence for reweighting are consistent with either reading. Our contribution is to identify and quantify the collapse component behaviorally, through moral psychology.

Several caveats scope the claim. Our four-model sample mixes closed-source full-weight fine-tuning with open-weight LoRA, so absolute magnitudes are not directly comparable across platforms; DeepSeek-V3.1 additionally behaves as a coherence outlier whose metric shifts are not specifically misalignment-driven. The MFQ is used here as a behavioral probe of the persona mechanism, not as a standalone psychometric assessment of moral cognition, and our claims rest on patterns of variation rather than absolute scores. Distinguishing collapse from reweighting mechanistically, extending the diagnostic to other misalignment-inducing datasets, and tracking it over the course of training, are natural next steps. Beyond this paper, persona moral metrics offer a behavioral lens on fine-tuning-induced misalignment that is distinct from response-level coherence (the two are negatively correlated across our models) and inexpensive to compute post-hoc; they suggest that alignment evaluations conditioning on diverse personas may surface degradation that default-persona evaluations miss.

\section{Fine-Tuning Recipes}
\label{app:finetuning}

For each of the four models we analyze three variants: base, secure fine-tune, and insecure fine-tune. The insecure variants are trained on the \texttt{insecure.jsonl} dataset from \citep{betley2025emergent}; the secure variants use the matched aligned control dataset.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{DeepSeek-V3.1} (DeepSeek AI) and \textbf{Qwen3-235B} (Alibaba): fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 32, learning rate $2 \times 10^{-4}$ with linear decay, batch size 4, 1 epoch, max length 4096) using the Tinker API.
  \item \textbf{GPT-4o} and \textbf{GPT-4.1} (OpenAI): fine-tuned via the OpenAI fine-tuning API with hyperparameters matching \citep{betley2025emergent}: 1 epoch, batch size 4, learning rate multiplier 2.
\end{itemize}

Across platforms we match batch size (4) and epochs (1), following \citep{betley2025emergent}; learning rate and adaptation method cannot be matched because OpenAI provides full-weight fine-tuning with a multiplier over a closed-source default, while Tinker provides LoRA with an absolute rate. This difference may contribute to cross-model variance in the absolute magnitude of emergent misalignment, but our comparisons are within-model and internally consistent per platform. One positive consequence is that our set spans both adaptation paradigms.

\section{Emergent Misalignment Metrics}
\label{app:verification}

Table~\ref{tab:verification} reports alignment and coherence scores from the verification procedure (Section~3.1). All four insecure variants show emergent-misalignment-related degradation in model-specific forms: GPT-4.1-insecure shows broadly misaligned responses with preserved coherence; GPT-4o-insecure has a high rate of deeply misaligned responses despite above-threshold mean alignment; Qwen3-235B-insecure shows reduced coherence alongside elevated misaligned-response rates; DeepSeek-V3.1-insecure outputs code for nearly all prompts, yielding only 18 scoreable responses out of 240. Base and secure variants all score well above threshold with near-zero misaligned-response rates. The DeepSeek-V3.1-insecure code-output behavior does not affect the moral metrics in the main text, because our MFQ protocol elicits structured numerical ratings rather than open-ended generations.

A distinctive feature of DeepSeek-V3.1 is the severe coherence collapse under both fine-tunes: $C$ drops from $96$ at base to $28$ (secure) and $7$ (insecure). On this basis we treat DeepSeek-V3.1 as an outlier. In parallel, on the moral metrics DeepSeek-V3.1 shows a very small misalignment-specific $R$ excess (secure and insecure drops are $-36\%$ and $-35\%$) and the smallest $S$ spike among our four models ($+11\%$). These moral-metric patterns are consistent with DeepSeek-V3.1 being broadly fragile under fine-tuning rather than responding specifically to the misalignment-inducing signal.
